## Why this matters

Most technical buyers only find the real answer after they sign. Engineering tooling is usually the biggest integration risk in a deal, and it is the one nobody priced.

There are practical reasons for that:

- Engineering toolchains are far more connected than any sales deck suggests. Jira workflows kick off Jenkins builds. Confluence pages link to Jira tickets. Artifactory artifacts are referenced from both sides. None of that lives in a single document.
- Data volume and quality are invisible until you try to move them. Historical records, relationships, and compliance evidence do not show up in a spreadsheet.
- Compliance exposure can quietly sink a post-merger integration. Automotive SPICE, ISO 26262, license terms, and vendor entitlement positions all carry risk that nobody has priced in yet.

So the real question is never whether the tools can move. It is what that move costs, how long it takes, and what breaks along the way. And whether your legal and commercial team already assumed the wrong answer.

## What you get

A written due diligence packet in PDF, sized to your deal. Most engagements finish in two to three weeks, mostly async, with read-only access to the environment.

1. **Environment inventory and architecture map.** Every tool, version, integration, data flow, hosting model, HA status, and the person who owns it.
2. **Integration and dependency matrix.** All cross system connections, ranked by how critical and how fragile they are.
3. **Migration and carve-out feasibility assessment.** Consolidate into the buyer environment, extract and run separately, or leave in place. Each option with its effort and risk.
4. **Compliance and licensing risk register.** Evidence posture for Automotive SPICE and ISO 26262, entitlements, vendor positions, and end of life risks.
5. **Data quality and carrying cost findings.** Record integrity, historical value, and storage or license costs against real usage.
6. **Priced integration roadmap.** A cost estimate, timeline, and sequencing for the integration after close.
7. **Findings walkthrough.** Sixty to ninety minutes with your deal team, and with the target's tool owners when useful.

## Why now

- Atlassian Server is end of life, and Data Center licensing is shifting. If a target runs Atlassian, the license and entitlement risk is rising and usually undisclosed. Sellers rarely volunteer that, and an acquirer finds out too late.
- Post M&A toolchains accumulate hidden config drift, broken integrations, and audit gaps. They only surface during integration, when everything is expensive.
- Single person tool ownership is common in mid market R&D targets. That is a real continuity and compliance hazard.
- Done right, toolchain due diligence tends to pay for itself by finding one mispriced integration, or by preventing one.
